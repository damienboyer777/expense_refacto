Let's be honest about the moat

What's actually defensible

Not the moat

Being "linked to payroll", on its own

For ordinary out-of-pocket spend, the payslip line only exists because the employee fronts the cash. It's a by-product of a friction — and the market is busy removing that friction. We shouldn't sell it as an asset.

The real moat

Two things no spend tool can copy

  • The payroll engine + SMB base + distribution. Expense rides on top of it.
  • The payroll-native items: mileage (IK), meal allowances, benefits in kind — anything under social contributions. They must be processed in payroll; URSSAF treatment is non-trivial; a card doesn't remove that.

Our edge isn't "we connect to payroll" — it's owning the payroll-native slice no spend tool can touch.

A direction to explore together

A strategic fork to explore

Lean out → spend

Go card-first

  • Remove the advance, capture spend in real time, monetize via interchange.
  • Build via BaaS (Treezor, Swan, Stripe Issuing) or partner with an existing player (Qonto).
Trade-off: decouples general spend from payroll; adds regulatory & ops load.
Lean in → payroll-native depth

Deepen where we're unique

  • IK / mileage, allowances, benefits, real-time cumulation, compliance (e-invoicing).
  • Cards stay out of scope, or partner-only.
Trade-off: smaller surface; doesn't answer the "spend platform" market move.

Both pull on the same resource — the payroll link. Worth exploring as directions, not a verdict — and AI is the cross-cutting lever on either path.
