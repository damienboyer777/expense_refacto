03 · The thesis

What's actually defensible

Key takeaway

The payroll link isn't an asset in itself — it's a consequence of advancing funds. E-cards remove the advance, so they decouple from payroll. "Extend the payroll loop" and "card-first" are therefore antagonistic — and "AI in processing" is a commodity (Mindee already does it).

The moat

Engine, base & distribution

The payroll engine, the SMB base and the distribution muscle — hard to copy, and ours.

No card replaces this

Payroll-native items

Mileage (IK), meal allowances, benefits in kind, contributable expenses — they must run through payroll for URSSAF treatment.

OCR is table stakes. The defensible ground is the payroll-native core — and, separately, the chance to build a real card product on top of our distribution.

03 · The thesis

The decision underneath it all

Before the build question, one positioning question conditions everything else.

Lens 1

HR · employee benefits

Expense as part of how we manage employee benefits and HR, sitting alongside payroll. Core target 1–250 employees.

Lens 2

Procurement · corporate spend

Expense as corporate spend management — closer to procurement, where new entrants attack. Wins specific needs (e.g. construction).

Question

Today reimbursement is a payroll variable. Cards take spend out of the payroll circuit. Do we want that decoupling? This is the call that frames lens, fork and team — and it belongs in this room, not in a follow-up.

The real question: not only "out of payroll" — it's which expenses stay payroll-native, which go spend-native, and which we simply reconcile.

04 · E-cards in focus

Sizing option B — the model to build

Two revenue streams from embedded cards
  • Interchange revenue share — a % of interchange, tiered 30 → 40% by monthly card spend
  • Net interest income share — a % of interest on deposits, tiered 20 → 30% by client count

Both ride high rates right now — interchange and interest are elevated, which lifts the case.

We can model it in-house

Extrapolate transactions / head × interchange (~€1–1.20/tx, our reverse share) × adoption, from data already in PayFit. No need to trust Swan/Qonto business plans.

Plausible — not proven
16k cards × 5 tx/mo × €1.50
≈ €1.44M / yr

Consistent with Swan's ~€1M opportunity — and with the ~1.5× revenue/employee (+30%) back-of-envelope.

Go/no-go depends on transaction volume per segment — the missing data, not Swan's estimate.

Vision ExpenseINTERNAL · 18
Annex · Closest analog (FR)

Lucca — the HR-native card, already shipping

What it is
  • A French HR/finance suite (SIRH). "Moyens de paiement" = a payment account + cards built into Lucca, next to Cleemy (expense reports)
  • Issued via Swan (BaaS) · Mastercard · funds secured at BNP — no banking licence of their own
  • Account & virtual cards are free (€0) → monetised on interchange, not subscription
  • Real-time limits, policy blocks, single-use cards generated only after manager approval, auto-reconciliation into Cleemy
The moat they market — and it's ours to claim
  • Native SIRH integration auto-deletes cards when an employee leaves
  • Cards blocked on absences — leave, paid holidays, sick days
  • They explicitly ding pure players for handling absences only "partially"

This is the strongest evidence that our HR/payroll context is an asset on the card itself — not just for reimbursement. It validates option B, and it's an urgency flag: a direct FR HRIS competitor already does it.

Annex · US payroll twin

Gusto — a payroll company that built a money layer

Employee side

Gusto Wallet

  • Spending account + Gusto Debit Card (Visa, via nbkc bank)
  • Early pay tied to the paycheck · on-demand pay (Clair)
  • Savings goals & budgeting tools
Business side

Gusto Money

  • Bill Pay & Invoicing — money in/out, same place as payroll
  • Payroll Bridge line of credit
  • App lists "corporate cards + expense mgmt"
Model & monetisation

Built on banking partners (nbkc, Visa; Clair) — "a payroll company, not a bank". Tools included with the subscription; transaction fees apply, with a cash-management service charge (~$200/yr) and discounted rates as upsell.

Why it matters for us

The payroll-native → money path is proven at scale in the US, on partners, tied to the paycheck. Directional validation for B — and a reminder that employee wallet vs. corporate expense card are two different bets. For our 2026 decision, treat Gusto as long-term inspiration, not proof of go.
